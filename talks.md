Talks I've given, and the material I can bring to a room. Slides, notes, and demos live in public repos, so you can run them yourself.

## Talks given

### CYBR.SEC.CON, Houston <small>(September 2026)</small>

I helped run the CYBR.HAK.CON AI Village and gave two talks, plus a workshop built from them.

- **A Language That Compiles Differently Every Time.** Generative AI recompiles itself on every run, so the real work is the checks around it. Agreement is not verification. Ships with a small runnable demo.  
  [Writeup](/a-language-that-compiles-differently-every-time.html) | [Repo](https://github.com/Qweary/ai-village-2026)
- **Swarms That Fight and Fix Each Other in a Cage.** Red and blue AI swarms, turned loose on each other, and what you can and can't conclude from it.  
  [Writeup](/swarms-that-fight-and-fix-each-other-in-a-cage.html) | [Repo](https://github.com/Qweary/ai-village-2026)
- The trip itself: [Nobody Will Think You Ain't From Houston](/nobody-will-think-you-aint-from-houston.html)

### ToorCamp <small>(2026)</small>

- **Dead Bytes Tell No Lies.** My main-stage lock talk, right after the keynote, built on the T2/T3 research. Paired with a hands-on flash-chip workshop where people wrote their own code into a real lock and watched it open. I also ran a lock-drilling workshop that grew from one hour to two and a half.  
  [Writeup](/heard-it-on-the-pirate-radio.html) | [Repo](https://github.com/Qweary/deadbytes)

### CyberHakCon, Dallas <small>(May 2026)</small>

- **The AI Village workshop** <small>(May 27, 2026)</small>. Three browser demos, FORGE, COMBAT, and EVOLVE, and three labs. I helped run the AI Village, and my talk there was about picking AI models the way you pick a programming language.  
  [Writeup](/heard-it-on-the-pirate-radio.html) | [Repo](https://github.com/Qweary/CybrHakConDemos)
